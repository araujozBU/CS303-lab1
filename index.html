Halloween began over 2,000 years ago with the ancient Celtic festival of Samhain, when people lit bonfires and wore costumes to ward off wandering spirits. In the eighth century, the Christian church set November 1 as All Saints' Day, blending old traditions into a night known as All Hallows' Eve. Irish and Scottish immigrants brought these customs to North America in the 1800s, where they started carving glowing jack-o'-lanterns out of native pumpkins instead of turnips, turning the ancient holiday into the fun, modern celebration we know today.
